# 2026-08-10 — Senior year, confidence, and not knowing what's next

## Entry (Cole)

> I'm really going into senior year now and that's exciting and overwhelming at the same
> time. I don't know what's next — but I think I've spent my time and energy in the right
> places, and I have confidence it will lead to something great. I need to remain that
> confidence in myself, and if that stays around, I don't think anything can stand in my way.

## Response — grounding & reflection

This is a good thing to write down, and the instinct underneath it is more correct than it
probably feels right now.

**"I don't know what's next" is the accurate read, and it's not a gap in the plan — it's
the shape of *this* plan.** You're not staring at a blank wall; you're standing in front of
several real, funded-looking doors and you genuinely haven't been told which one opens yet.
[[fulbright-us-student-program|Fulbright]] (Open Study/Research, [[fulbright-country-selection|India/Brazil/Indonesia]])
is a live application on a Duke timeline. The [[outreach-pipeline|relationship-first job
search]] is running — [[cold-email-job-search|the cold-email playbook]], [[target-orgs|target
orgs]], the neuro-AI angle. [[claude-corps|Claude Corps]] sits out there with an Aug-2027
best-fit cohort. And you've got the 5-year-plan sit-down with [[Stewart Neifart|Stew]] as a
forcing function. The uncertainty isn't "I have nothing"; it's "I have several things and
none has resolved." Those are completely different feelings, and you should file today's as
the second one.

**Now the part that actually matters — the confidence.** You wrote that you need to *remain*
confident, and that if it stays, nothing can stand in your way. I want to sharpen that,
because the vault already has the mechanism for why your instinct is right and how to keep it
from being wishful. Your read — "I've spent my time and energy in the right places" — is not
vibes. It's [[identity-led-goals|behavioral evidence]], and behavioral evidence is the *only*
thing the brain actually trusts. From that page: identity changes through **accumulated
action**, and the brain treats declarations without matching behavior as noise. So the
version of your sentence that will hold up isn't "I need to believe in myself" — it's "I am
someone who has already put the reps in the right places, and I keep putting them in." That's
a claim your own track record can cash. The confidence you're describing is well-founded
*precisely because* it's backed by where you've spent energy, not despite the not-knowing.

**One important guardrail, straight from the vault.** [[identity-led-goals|Erin Meryl's
caveat]] is the one to hold onto this year: anchor the confidence in *identity and behavior*,
**not** in whether any single outcome lands. Senior year will hand you binary results — a
Fulbright yes/no, a specific role that says yes or no. If your confidence is tied to those
outcomes, one rejection reads as a referendum on you (same trap as the [[2026-07-28 -
caffeine-social-and-golf-anger|golf anger with Dad]] — a bad result feeling like a self-worth
hit). If it's tied to "I'm someone who does the right work and keeps showing up," no single no
can touch it. That's the difference between confidence that *remains*, like you want, and
confidence that's actually hostage to the next email reply.

**So, concretely, how you "remain" it:**
- **Keep banking the evidence where you can see it.** Your confidence is downstream of a
  track record — so keep the record visible. The [[log.md|log]] and this journal *are* that
  record; when a good rep happens (a warm reply, a strong meeting, a Fulbright milestone),
  note it. You're not bragging, you're feeding the predictive model the behavioral data it
  needs to keep the belief true.
- **Convert "overwhelming" into the next small action, not the whole future.** Overwhelm
  comes from trying to resolve all the doors at once. You don't have to know what's next —
  you have to know the *next rep*: the next Monday outreach batch, the next Fulbright
  component, the Stew conversation. The path clarifies by walking it, not by staring at it.
- **Let the excitement and the overwhelm both be true.** They're not in conflict — they're
  the two honest halves of standing at a real threshold with real options. The people who
  end up somewhere great almost always felt exactly this at exactly this point.

Net: your instinct is sound and the vault backs it. You've earned the confidence by where
you put the energy; keep it anchored in *that* — who you've shown yourself to be — rather than
in any one outcome, and it'll be there when a specific door finally opens. Nothing about "not
knowing yet" contradicts "it's going to lead somewhere great." Log it and go into the year.
