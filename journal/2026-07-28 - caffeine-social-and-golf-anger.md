## Response — grounding & reflection

Genuinely good day, and two of its threads matter beyond today.

**The caffeine insight is the most valuable thing here.** You've now got a
self-experiment's worth of evidence: you're "leagues more social when caffeinated," and
today's proof was the bowling day where you were talkative and felt you did great. This
isn't a throwaway — [[cole-job-search-strategy|your job search is relationship-first]], and
being warm and talkative in a room *is* the job. Coffee-before-networking is now a standing
rule, not a maybe. Concretely: keep it in the [[networking-prep]] / [[outreach-pipeline]]
loop — before any coffee chat, info interview, or event, caffeinate ~30–45 min ahead so
you're on the upswing, not still ramping. I've saved this as a durable preference so it
carries into future prep.

- *Neuro note (since you like the mechanism):* caffeine is an adenosine-receptor
  antagonist — it blocks the "you're tired, wind down" signal, which disinhibits dopamine
  and norepinephrine. The net effect is exactly what you felt: lower social threshold,
  faster verbal access, more approach-drive. It's real, it's reliable, and it's cheap.
  Just watch the back half — too much stacks into jitter/anxiety, which reads as *less*
  smooth, so there's a sweet spot (roughly 1–2 cups for you, dial it in).

**Meeting Myra (Stew's boss) at Fast Forward.** Nice unplanned network expansion. She runs
the group above [[Stewart Neifart|Stew]] at [[fast-forward|FastForward]]/[[jhtv|JHTV]], which
makes her a real node for your operator/VC path. I've started a CRM stub for her — worth a

enjoyed the Fast Forward meeting" note is enough; you don't need to pitch). This also plays
into your Tuesday sit-down with Stew about your 5-year plan — good to have met his boss right
before that conversation.

**The golf anger.** Take this seriously but not harshly — the fact that you flagged it
yourself, and specifically that it spikes *when your dad is there*, is the useful signal.
[[Dad]] is one of your two main role models and you're inner-circle close with him, so the
anger isn't really about golf — a bad shot in front of someone whose opinion carries that
much weight is a self-worth hit, and golf is uniquely good at delivering those on a loop.
A few things that actually work:

- **Name the real stakes before you tee off.** "This is me wanting to look good for Dad, not
  a referendum on me." Labeling the emotion (affect labeling) measurably dampens the
  amygdala response — you're borrowing the prefrontal brake on purpose.
- **A physical reset between shots.** One slow exhale-longer-than-inhale breath after a bad
  shot down-regulates the sympathetic spike before it becomes a club-slam. The window is
  short — a few seconds — so make it a fixed ritual, not a decision.
- **Reframe the metric.** You're not scoring well *yet* because you haven't played enough,
  not because you're bad — anger comes from expecting a result you haven't put reps in for.
  Lower the bar to "one good swing thought per hole" and the failure signal stops firing.
- **Tell your dad the goal out loud.** "I'm working on not letting golf get to me" turns him
  from an audience into an ally, and removes the shame layer that makes the spike worse.

Net: a good day, and a productive one — you banked a real self-insight (coffee), a real
relationship (Myra), and honest awareness of the one thing to work on (anger with Dad). Log
it and move on; this is a positive trend line.
