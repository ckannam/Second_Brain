# Lower Motor Neurons and Segmental Reflexes — Tutorial Notes (Week 9)

*Raw source for [[neurosci-206-lower-motor-neurons]]. Aligned to Neuroscience 7e Ch 16.*

## Map to Neuroscience Core Concepts
- NCC1. The brain is the body's most complex organ.
- NCC3. Genetically determined circuits are the foundation of the nervous system.

## Key Concepts
- **16.1** Interacting subsystems in the CNS make essential and distinct contributions to motor control.
- **16.2** Lower motor neurons in the spinal cord and brainstem map the body's musculature.
- **16.3** Motor units of varying size produce appropriate movements.
- **16.4** Local circuitry mediates reflexes that rapidly adjust muscle tension in response to sensory input.
- **16.5** Local circuitry coordinates the output of lower motor neurons for rhythmic, stereotyped behavior.
- **16.6** Damage to lower motor neurons results in the "lower motor neuron syndrome."

## Outline (condensed from the pasted tutorial)
### 16.1 — Four motor subsystems
1. **Lower motor neurons** = α and γ motor neurons of brainstem + spinal cord; efferent innervation of skeletal muscle of head/body. Conventionally, only neurons that directly innervate NMJs of striated muscle are "LMNs." "Lower motor circuits" also includes local interneurons. (Autonomic postganglionic = "visceral motor neurons.")
2. **Spinal cord circuits** = "final common pathway" — segmental reflexes (myotatic) + intersegmental reflexes (CPGs).
3. **Descending control systems:** pyramidal (volitional, motor cortex → pyramidal tract); extrapyramidal (brainstem reticular formation, vestibular nuclei, superior colliculus → posture/orienting; limbic → motivated movement); visceral/autonomic.
4. **Modulatory systems:** basal ganglia (select/initiate/suppress motor programs, habit) + cerebellum (on-line adjustment via proprioception, skill learning).

### 16.2 — LMN distribution & somatotopy
- Spinal cord: α + γ motor neurons in the **ventral horn**. Motor neuronal pool for a muscle = rod-shaped column spanning several segments.
- Somatotopy: cervical/lumbar enlargements → arms/legs. Across ventral horn: **medial = axial/proximal**, **lateral = distal extremity**.
- Brainstem: motor neurons in cranial-nerve nuclei (dorsal-medial somatic motor; paramedian branchial motor).

### 16.3 — Motor units
- Motor unit = an α motor neuron + the muscle fibers it innervates. Three types: **slow**, **fast fatigue-resistant**, **fast fatigable**.
- **Size principle:** slow recruited first → fast fatigue-resistant → fast fatigable. Use-dependent plasticity (endurance "slows" units; resistance training speeds tension generation).

### 16.4 — Segmental sensorimotor reflexes
- **Myotatic (muscle spindle) reflex** — monitors/maintains muscle LENGTH. Spindle = intrafusal fibers in parallel with extrafusal; **nuclear bag** (mostly phasic/dynamic) + **nuclear chain** (tonic/static) fibers. **Group Ia** encircles both; **group II** → chain + static bag. Central effects: monosynaptic excitation of homonymous + synergist α-MNs; **disynaptic inhibition of antagonist** α-MNs (reciprocal innervation). Also feeds cerebellum (spinocerebellar) + cortex (DCML, conscious proprioception).
  - **Gain** = extrafusal contraction per unit stretch (output/input). Set by **γ motor neurons** (contract intrafusal ends → reset spindle sensitivity; prevents "unloading" during shortening). Context-dependent (↑ before difficult/explosive tasks; descending + presynaptic GABAergic modulation of Ia terminals).
  - **Muscle tone** = resting tension; depends on resting α-MN firing (driven by spindle afferents; γ sets gain). Hypotonia = LMN sign; hypertonia = UMN sign.
- **Golgi tendon organ reflex** — monitors/maintains muscle FORCE/tension; insensitive to stretch. GTO = **group Ib** endings in collagen at muscle–tendon junction, **in series**. Central effects: **disynaptic inhibition of homonymous** α-MN; disynaptic excitation of antagonist. Tonic Ib keeps steady force; protects against excessive tension.
- **Flexion / crossed-extension reflex** — withdraw limb from noxious stimulus. Sensor: **group Aδ** nociceptors. Ipsilateral: disynaptic excitation of flexors + inhibition of extensors. Contralateral: polysynaptic excitation of extensors + inhibition of flexors (postural support).

### 16.5 — Central pattern generators (CPGs)
- Spinal circuits coordinate rhythmic limb movement (walk/run/swim); brainstem CPGs for respiration, chewing, swallowing.
- Local interneuron circuits spanning a few segments; some neurons are pacemakers; initiated by descending or sensory input; one circuit can make different rhythms.
- Cat: spinal transection above lumbar enlargement → hindlimbs still walk on a treadmill; survives dorsal-root section → pattern is "wired in," not just reflex. Humans rely more on descending control.

### 16.6 — Lower motor neuron syndrome
- Neural: paralysis/paresis, profound hyporeflexia/areflexia, loss of tone.
- Muscle: atrophy (months to manifest), fibrillations (denervated fibers) + fasciculations (abnormal motor-unit activity).

## Study questions (professor's own) + answers
1. What kinds of neurons innervate skeletal muscle? → **D. neurons in the ventral horn of the spinal cord.**
2. Make a fist, squeeze harder. What happened? → **B. recruited additional LARGER motor units** (size principle).
3. Make a fist, squeeze harder. What happened? → **C. Ib afferent activity increased substantially** (GTO senses contraction/tension, not stretch).
4. What most directly increases the gain of the myotatic reflex? → **A. increase in firing of gamma motor neurons.**
5. Which behaviors are mediated by CPGs? → **H. all of the above** (walking, running, swimming, scratching, laughing, chewing, breathing).
6. Which sign would make you think the patient did NOT have a lower motor neuron injury? → **D. increased myotatic reflexes** (hyperreflexia = UMN sign).

*Answers: 1=D, 2=B, 3=C, 4=A, 5=H, 6=D.*
