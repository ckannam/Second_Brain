# Duke OTC — Office for Translation & Commercialization

Duke's **technology-transfer core** — the office that protects, markets, and licenses innovations
made by Duke inventors, and decides whether to **license to an existing company** or **spin out a
new one**. Led by **[[Robin Rasor]]** (Associate VP for Translation & Commercialization; former
**AUTM president**, ex-Director of Licensing at Ohio State). The Duke counterpart to [[jhtv|JHTV]]'s
core — but framed **IP-management / licensing-first**, where JHTV brands itself **capital-first**.
Part of the broader [[duke-innovation-ecosystem]]. Robin **guest-lectured in Cole's class**
([[business-of-science-syllabus-spring2026|The Business of Science]], Wk 3).

## Structure
- **Licensing** — protect (patents), market, and license Duke IP.
- **New Ventures group** — helps faculty/staff explore starting companies around their inventions.
- **Government non-dilutive support** — SBIR/STTR guidance, I-Corps-style customer discovery
  (the [[valley-of-death]] bridge on the federal side).

## By the numbers
- **FY23:** helped spin out **15 startups**; Duke inventions returned **$102M** to inventors + the
  university.
- **FY21:** **13 startups** launched; **$90M+** in revenue.

## The structural read (for the [[kelli-luginbuhl-call-prep|Kelli conversation]])
OTC is a strong **licensing + new-ventures** shop, but it's **one unit in a federated Duke
system** — the *capital* layer lives in a separate entity ([[duke-capital-partners]]), the
education/culture layer in [[duke-ie|Duke I&E]], and medical de-risking in [[duke-ctsi-medx|CTSI /
MEDx]]. Contrast JHTV, where tech transfer + accelerator + translational funding + a **dedicated
capital-strategy team** sit inside **one integrated "front door."** See [[duke-innovation-ecosystem]].
