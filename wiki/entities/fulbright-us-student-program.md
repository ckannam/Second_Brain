# Fulbright U.S. Student Program

The U.S. government's flagship international exchange fellowship. Cole is applying for the **2027-2028 grant year** (9 months, starting after his Duke graduation), on his aunt's suggestion. Hub for his application; see the **[[Fulbright]]** bucket.

## Cole's application at a glance
- **Award type:** **Open Study/Research** — Cole proposes his own project (not an English Teaching Assistantship / ETA).
- **Project:** [[ai-lowering-entrepreneurship-barriers|how AI is lowering the barriers — capital, expertise, infrastructure — to founding a company]] in economies where that wasn't previously possible. Grounded in his **[[jhtv|JHTV]]** VC-relations + [[translational-funding]] work, *not* his neuro/chem lab research (reviewers still weigh professional background outside the major).
- **Country:** one focused country-specific proposal required — shortlist India / Brazil / Indonesia in [[fulbright-country-selection]]; commit once a host responds.
- **Affiliated institution:** **[[Duke]]** (selected in the apply.iie.org portal).

## Application components (for the Aug 21 Duke review)
1. Fulbright Online Application account (apply.iie.org), Duke as affiliated institution.
2. **Statement of Grant Purpose** — max 2 single-spaced pages; the core proposal.
3. **Personal Statement** — 1 single-spaced page; personal/intellectual narrative.
4. **Three Short Answers** — Flexibility & Adaptability · Community Engagement · Impact of the Fulbright Award.
5. Official transcripts (Duke + any other institutions).
6. **Three letters of recommendation** (registered in portal). Plan: JHTV supervisor + a Duke professor + one more for adaptability. **Hard deadline Sept 10** (see below).
7. Host **affiliation/invitation letter** — encouraged, not strictly mandatory (USIEF/AMINEF/etc. can help place if none secured).
8. Language Self-Evaluation Form — optional; strengthens the app if committing to language study.

Items 2–4 are where **the aunt** helps (writing/narrative review). Duke's two GA reviewers — [[Cyprene Caines]] (former Fulbright scholar) and [[Clayton Beasley]] — also review drafts, coordinated by adviser [[Anna Bernard-Hoverstad]].

## Deadlines (authoritative — from [[anna-fulbright-duke-2026-07-14]])
- **Fri Aug 21, 12:00pm noon ET** — Duke campus deadline: **lock / "Ready for Review"** in the portal (do NOT submit).
- **Thu Sept 10, 12:00pm noon ET** — recommendation letters + foreign-language evals due.
- **Tue Oct 6, 4:59pm ET** — final national submission.

## Duke endorsement (reassuring)
Duke endorses **all** affiliated applicants — **not competitive, not capped**. NCS uploads a strengths evaluation to national reviewers; after the campus deadline they unlock apps (by Sept 2) so applicants keep polishing, hold an informational interview in September, and submit by Oct 6. Working with NCS is optional and penalty-free.

## Funding
Not a lump sum — a **monthly living stipend** sized to in-country cost of living (**~$1,700/mo for 9 months**, India-specific estimate; varies by country). Plus round-trip international **airfare**, a **settling-in allowance**, a **research allowance**, and **health/accident insurance**.

## Competition structure
You compete only against applicants to your **same country AND same award type** — reinforcing why Cole must commit to one country and why award rates by country matter (see [[fulbright-country-selection]]).
