# Interleaving (mixed practice)

Shuffling the order of practice problems across topics/skills — rather than **blocking** all
problems of one type together — improves retention and transfer. Closely tied to the
[[spacing-effect]] (interleaving forces spacing as a side effect) but adds its own mechanism:
**discriminative contrast**, where juxtaposing problem types teaches you to *tell them apart* and
pick the right method, not just execute a known one.

## Evidence
- Small-to-medium effect for mathematics: g = 0.34 (Brunmair & Richter 2019), cited in
  [[spacing-math-meta-analysis-murray-2025]].
- Most math textbooks are laid out **blocked** (all of one type together), which is exactly the
  weaker arrangement (Rohrer et al. 2020).

## Why it matters here
Interleaving is the retrieval modality for the **[[knowledge-types|skill]]** bucket: spaced,
*mixed* practice problems the learner can't solve on autopilot. For a
[[neuro-channel|learning app]], "generate an interleaved problem set from this material" is the
concrete Skill-bucket action — and it's what the math evidence supports over flashcards. See
[[retrieval-practice]] for how modality should match knowledge type.
