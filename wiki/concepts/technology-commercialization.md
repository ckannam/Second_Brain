# Technology Commercialization (the frameworks Cole learned)

The set of frameworks for moving a technology **from the lab to the market** — the spine of
[[Kelli Luginbuhl]] & [[Doug Speight]]'s Duke course
[[business-of-science-syllabus-spring2026|The Business of Science]] (I&E 710). This is the
academic vocabulary behind everything Cole does at [[capital-strategy|JHTV]]; the concepts below
are the *transferable takeaways* to name in the [[kelli-luginbuhl-call-prep|Kelli conversation]].

## The most useful concepts (ranked by transfer value)
1. **[[valley-of-death|The valley of death]]** — *(Cole's #1)* the gap between research funding and
   private investment where tech stalls; the whole point of non-dilutive funding is to bridge it.
2. **Stakeholder landscape + misaligned incentives** — scientists, universities, government,
   investors, and customers each have *different motivations*, and commercialization fails as
   often from **misaligned incentives / human behavior** as from bad science. (Course themes:
   "impacts of complex human behavior," and the Week-5 VC panel exercise: *analyze how misaligned
   incentives conflict with commercialization goals.*) The most Kelli-and-Doug idea in the course.
3. **Commercialization phases** — commercialization is a **staged** process, not an event; you
   match the right capital and the right actions to the current stage.
4. **University Tech Transfer Offices (TTOs) & Bayh-Dole logic** — how universities license IP and
   what incentives drive them. Direct compare: [[Duke OTC]] (Robin Rasor guest-lectured) vs.
   [[jhtv|JHTV]]'s capital-first model.
5. **Government non-dilutive funding — SBIR/STTR** — the federal programs that fund early
   de-risking; part of the [[translational-funding]] escalator.
6. **Transactional intelligence** — investor motivations (VC vs. **strategics** vs. other capital)
   and how the **transaction cycle shapes team dynamics** — reading the deal, not just the tech.
7. **Tech / market / execution risk triad** — articulate a venture's risk in three buckets; the
   backbone of a commercialization brief.
8. **Customer discovery & market assessment** — market size, trends, competitive landscape; you
   validate demand, not assume it.
9. **The commercialization brief + H5W framing** — distill the plan into an executive summary
   (the course's "H5W" framework for structuring the go-to-market brief). *(Exact H5W definition
   not in the syllabus — confirm with Cole/Canvas.)*
10. **AI-assisted invention-disclosure scouting** — using AI to triage an IP portfolio down to
    high-potential technologies. **This is literally what Cole's [[vc-matching-second-brain]]
    does** — the course taught the manual version of his tool.
