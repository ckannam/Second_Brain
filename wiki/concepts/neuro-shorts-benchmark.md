# Neuro Shorts benchmark — what works in neuro/psych short-form

Reference research for the **[[neuro-channel|Neuro]]** channel: how the biggest neuroscience/
psychology explainer channels structure their short-form videos, plus the data-backed hook/
pacing rules for YouTube Shorts. Feeds the [[neuro-scripts-batch-1|script drafts]] and the
production pipeline. Compiled 2026-07-29 from web research (sources at bottom); treat
subscriber counts as a 2026 snapshot.

## Channels to study (the comparison set)

| Channel | Scale | Format | Why it's a model for Neuro |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Psych2Go** | ~14M+ (largest in the space) | **Animated** chibi / "marshmallow-people" characters, voice-over narration (narrator heard, not seen), bite-sized | **Closest analog to Neuro's doodle-scene plan** — proves an animated-character explainer with listicle titles scales huge. Founder Tai Khuong studied psych at UBC. |
| **BrainCraft** (Vanessa Hill) | ~617K | Stop-motion + paper-craft animation, host on camera | Craft/hand-made aesthetic (like Neuro's hand-drawn doodles) as a brand differentiator |
| **Neuro Transmissions** (Alie Astrocyte + Micah) | mid-size | Neuroscientist + therapist duo, "the brain, simply" | Credibility-led positioning; explains mechanisms in plain language — Neuro's exact lane |
| **SciShow Psych** (Hank Green / Brit Garner) | ~863K | Host-on-camera, studies-explained, historical context | Rigor + citing real studies; shows the "science *is* the marketing" model |
| **Neuro Lifestyle** | ~518K | Neuroscience-for-self-improvement | Overlaps Neuro's "understand your brain to live better" mission |

**Read for Cole:** the animated-narrator lane (Psych2Go) is both the most proven *and* the
cheapest to produce at Neuro's $0/AI-doodle stack — it doesn't need a host on camera. The
hand-drawn look (BrainCraft) is a legitimate differentiator, not a limitation. Positioning
that wins in this niche = **plain-language mechanism + a real study + an accessible mascot.**

## Title patterns that repeat

- **Listicle / number:** "*7 Signs of a Fake Apology*", "*5 Myths About Mental Illness*" —
  Psych2Go's staple. Number in the title sets a completion promise.
- **Second-person "why you…" question:** "*Why you walk into a room and forget*",
  "*Why you see your new car everywhere*" — the format the [[neuro-channel]] topic bank
  already uses. Makes it about the viewer.
- **Keyword-aligned:** as of 2026, Shorts has its own search filter — front-load the concrete
  keyword ("dopamine", "memory", "habit") so the title is findable, not just clickable.

## Hook + pacing rules (data-backed)

The gates that decide whether a Short gets distributed:

- **The 3-second gate.** ~50–60% of viewers who drop off do so **within the first 3 seconds**.
  Your strongest line/visual must land at **~2.5–3s** — not after an intro. No logo cold-open.
- **The 15-second gate.** The 15-sec retention mark drives *sustained* distribution. Structure
  so there's a second payoff/turn by ~15s.
- **Length:** viral Shorts cluster at **20–35s**; **education tolerates 35–45s** *if the payoff
  is clear* (roughly +5–10s of grace vs. entertainment).
- **Hook types that work for science/education:**
  - **Curiosity gap** — incomplete statement/question that creates tension ("Your brain deletes
    the memory on purpose. Here's why.").
  - **Direct promise** — tell them exactly what they'll know by the end.
  - **Proof-driven** — lead with a study result or an on-screen number; education audiences
    reward evidence and on-screen data.
- **Craft:** tight cuts (a new visual every few seconds — the doodle-scene cadence), **captions
  for sound-off viewing** (kinetic captions already planned), and **one simple CTA** at the end.

## How Neuro should apply this

1. **Hook at 2.5s**, no intro card — open on the relatable moment ("You walk into a room… and
   forget why you came in").
2. **Second-person question title**, keyword front-loaded.
3. **One mechanism, one study/number** as the proof beat (~mid-video, before the 15s gate).
4. **~35–45s**, ~10–14 doodle scenes, kinetic captions, one CTA ("follow for one brain thing a day").
5. Steal Psych2Go's **animated-narrator economics**, BrainCraft's **hand-made look**, and
   Neuro Transmissions' **plain-language-mechanism** positioning.
