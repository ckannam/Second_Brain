# Cognitive biases

The subconscious runs on **heuristics** — shortcuts that process information fast. Usually they
work (they kept ancestors alive on split-second calls), but in modern contexts they produce
systematic errors. They're **features of subconscious processing optimizing for speed over
accuracy, not character flaws.** From [[conscious-subconscious-mind-ixcarus]].

- **Confirmation bias** — seek info that confirms existing beliefs, filter out the rest (the
  [[reticular-activating-system]] enforcing this). Why opposite camps see the same event
  differently.
- **Availability heuristic** — judge likelihood by how easily examples come to mind (recent
  plane-crash news → flying *feels* riskier though stats are unchanged).
- **Anchoring** — the first number seen becomes the reference point (crossed-out "original
  price" next to the sale price).
- **Sunk-cost fallacy** — keep investing in failing situations to avoid admitting a past choice
  was wrong ("already put in so much time").
- **Fundamental attribution error** — others' failures → their character; your failures → your
  circumstances. Protects self-image.

**The only counter is conscious awareness** — catching the shortcut and questioning it — which is
metabolically expensive ([[conscious-vs-subconscious]]), so biases persist. Relevant to any
decision Cole wants to make well; connects to [[predictive-processing]] and, in the AI domain,
mirrors why [[llm-as-judge|LLM judges]] and [[adversarial-code-review|adversarial review]] exist
— an outside checker catches shortcuts the first pass can't see in itself.
