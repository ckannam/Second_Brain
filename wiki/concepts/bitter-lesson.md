# The Bitter Lesson

Richard Sutton's argument (the "father of reinforcement learning"): over the long run,
methods that **leverage more data and compute** beat methods where humans hand-code
knowledge and constraints up front. You can spend your effort encoding your own cleverness
into a system, but scaling learning/search wins.

Cited in [[how-we-claude-code]] as the rationale for a working practice: **the more capable
the model, the more you should resist constraining it.** Corollary — "Claude is likely
better at extracting what you want from you than you are at specifying it," so let it
[[html-over-markdown-specs|interview you]] into a spec rather than over-specifying the
outcome. This underpins the broader shift toward [[outcome-oriented-agents|outcome-oriented]]
work and [[natural-language-coding]].
