# Conversational Presence — Cole's Operating Model & Growth Front

Takeaways from a long morning conversation with Claude (2026-08-01). Two halves: **how Cole
reads people** (his operating model) and **who he's trying to become in a room** (the growth
work + the tools). Everything here is **[said]** by Cole in that conversation.

## His operating model of people
- **The subconscious drives behavior far more than the conscious mind.** People run mostly on
  wiring, not deliberate reasoning. Grounds directly in [[conscious-vs-subconscious]] — the tiny
  ~7±2-item conscious workspace riding an ~11M-bits/sec subconscious.
- **People are wired to be self-focused.** Self-interest is the default setting, not a character
  flaw.
- **Once you understand someone's incentives, you can almost always predict them.** Incentives →
  behavior is his working model of people; echoes [[predictive-processing]] and the shortcut
  logic of [[cognitive-biases]].
- **He finds that predictability both useful and genuinely frustrating.** Useful because it lets
  him read a room and anticipate; frustrating because people rarely surprise him.
- **He rarely blames individuals — he sees the wiring behind their actions.** A compassionate,
  mechanistic read: behavior is caused, not chosen, so blame mostly misses the point. This is the
  interpersonal version of his [[neuroscience-of-behavior|grounding-behavior-in-neuro]] instinct.

## Who he's working toward becoming (in a room)
- **Talk less in groups.**
- **Lead with genuine curiosity** rather than his own take.
- **Pivot the spotlight onto others** instead of holding it.
- **Become a far better listener.**
- **Break free of his own predictable patterns** — apply the same incentive-reading he uses on
  others to catch and interrupt his own defaults.

## The four tools we landed on
1. **The half-second of silence** — a beat before responding, so he isn't running his own
   predictable pattern. (Interrupt the automatic reply; make room for the deliberate one.)
2. **Turn his excitement into questions** — when he's energized about something, convert the urge
   to broadcast into an urge to ask. Channels the very energy that usually makes him talk.
3. **A tight two-sentence pitch** — say who he is in two sentences, then stop, so the conversation
   can open back up to the other person. (Pairs with the [[cole-cold-email-template|3 credential
   sentences]] but tuned for a live room, not an email.)
4. **Be the rare person who actually asks about others** — he called this his **quiet superpower.**
   Most people don't do it; being the one who does is disproportionately memorable.

## Why this matters for Cole
His job search is **relationship-first** ([[growth-plan]], [[Job Search]]) — networking is the
compounding move, and he's [[cole-caffeine-social|much more talkative when caffeinated]]. The risk
of "more talkative" is talking *past* people; these four tools are the counterweight. The quiet
superpower — asking about others — is exactly what turns a caffeinated, energized Cole from
*impressive* into *memorable and liked*. It's the interpersonal complement to the discipline work
in [[discipline-without-willpower]] and the identity framing of [[identity-led-goals]] ("I am
someone who asks about others").
