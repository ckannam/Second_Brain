## 1. Why this fits ATP specifically (what the memo author didn't know)

The memo was written with limited understanding of ATP. These are the connections the vault adds:

- **ATP already has an aircrew-readiness Navy angle, and the 711th HPW *is* aircrew readiness.** ATP's
  Navy SBIR is the **"Aircrew Readiness Fingerprint"** (per [[atp-competitive-analysis]]) — a proposal
  ATP *just submitted* (pending, pre-award; Cole, Oct 2026). The 711th Human Performance Wing folds in
  the **USAF School of Aerospace Medicine** and runs an **Integrated Cockpit Sensing System** for
  aircrew physiological monitoring. Even as a *pending* proposal, ATP's aircrew-readiness work is a
  credible talking point for an Air Force conversation — not a cold start. The memo flagged the now-closed Navy
  topic **DON26BZ05-NV068 "Intelligent Tools for Naval Aircrew Performance and Readiness"** and DARPA
  **DPA26BZ03-DV012 "Engineering Sleep for Cognitive Performance"** as watch-for-reposts; both are
  squarely in ATP's aircrew lane.
- **ATP's product maps onto the DoD's #1 readiness problem: musculoskeletal (MSK) injury.** ATP is
  single-camera markerless pose → ~18 joint angles → individualized baseline → mobility/symmetry
  deviation over time (see [[athletic-training-portal]], [[atp-competitive-analysis]]). The memo says
  MSK injuries hit **>800,000 Service members annually** and that MTEC/CDMRP are paying for exactly
  this. ATP is a readiness/injury-risk tool by construction.
- **ATP's biggest competitive liability is also its federal framing requirement — and they're the same
  reframe.** The competitive analysis flags that *"predict injuries before they happen"* is FDA
  device-line risk (the WHOOP warning-letter trap) and must be reframed to wellness-safe
  **"deviation-from-baseline monitoring."** That identical reframe is what keeps ATP on the right side
  of **xTech's Army-medical-command exclusion** (clinical/rehab medicine is out) and the general DoD
  preference for a **commander-facing readiness tool**, not a medical device. One discipline solves
  two problems.
- **Founder/advisor fit is a federal credential, not just a sports one.** CEO [[Jaylen Coleman]]
  (ex-Duke football + BME) plus advisors **Dr. Dickens (military sports medicine)** and **Dr. Luck
  (Duke BME biomechanics)** — per the competitive analysis — map directly onto the DoD human-
  performance buyer. Dickens in particular is a military-credibility asset to put in front of 711 HPW,
  POTFF, and the xTech reviewers.
- **College-first + FERPA + NC-incorporated** (Duke football/baseball/wrestling, NCCU track) gives ATP
  the small-business profile and commercial-traction story these programs reward, and it sits inside
  **NC's own DoD human-performance ecosystem** — the memo names **NC-COMP** (>80 orgs, led by RTI,
  building human-performance tech for DoD), a natural local consortium for ATP to join.
- **JHTV / Johns Hopkins is the academic-lead partner these medical-research funders want.** MTEC and
  CDMRP/PRMRP awards favor an academic lead; Cole's [[capital-strategy|JHTV]] tie makes a Johns Hopkins
  clinical partner a realistic structure for the medical-research track (MTEC FA10B, PRMRP FY27).

## 4. Where ATP appears eligible (and the two real risks)

**Eligible, with caveats:**
- **xTech|Search 10:** requires a for-profit **US small business, >50% owned/controlled by US citizens
  or permanent residents, ≤500 employees, one submission per company.** ATP (pre-seed, NC-incorporated)
  clears the size/ownership bar today. ⚠️ **Two checks before submitting:**
  1. **Ownership drift.** If ATP has taken (or takes) [[dorm room fund]] or other VC money, confirm the
     **>50% US-citizen/PR ownership-and-control** test still holds. This is the same ownership question
     the DRF conflict flag already put on the table in [[athletic-training-portal]].
  2. **Navy-SBIR overlap disclosure.** xTech bars work "substantially the same" as other federally
     funded **or pending** work — and ATP's Navy "Aircrew Readiness Fingerprint" proposal is *pending*
     (just submitted). ATP **must disclose it as early as possible** and show how the Army readiness
     application differs in scope. (Pending counts; don't treat "not yet awarded" as "nothing to
     disclose.")
- **711 HPW CHEERS / AFWERX SBIR:** standard SBIR small-business eligibility — ATP qualifies. Note
  AFWERX requires a **CMMC Level 1/2 self-assessment** on applicable solicitations until Nov 10, 2026.
- **MTEC FA10B:** requires **MTEC membership + TRL≥3 + preliminary data** — the binding constraints, not
  eligibility. ATP's signed pilots are the preliminary-data source.

**Two risks that are ATP-specific (not generic):**
- **The medical/clinical line.** xTech *excludes* tech sitting **exclusively** in the Army Medical
  Research & Development Command portfolio (incl. military operational medicine, clinical/rehab
  medicine). ATP's "injury prediction" framing reads as clinical. **Mitigation = the same wellness-safe
  reframe the [[atp-competitive-analysis]] already prescribes:** position as **unit readiness /
  training-load / availability monitoring for commanders**, flagging deviations from an individual's
  baseline — not diagnosis or treatment.
- **TACFI/STRATFI does NOT apply to ATP's Navy work.** Those follow-on vehicles are **Department of the
  Air Force SBIR only**. ATP's Navy Phase II cannot convert to a TACFI/STRATFI — the way in is to *first*
  win a DAF (AFWERX / 711 HPW) SBIR, then scale it. This is a reason to open the 711 HPW relationship
  now rather than treating the Navy work as a path to Air Force scaling money.

> ✅ **Resolved (Cole, Oct 2026): ATP just *submitted* the Navy SBIR application — it is a pending
> proposal, pre-award.** So ATP is very early: not a Phase II awardee, not even a confirmed Phase I
> awardee yet. **This re-orders the memo's advice** — the Navy-STP / Phase-III / TACFI-STRATFI vehicles
> it leaned on are all *downstream* and don't apply now. Near-term, the right federal moves are exactly
> the relationship-and-prize plays below (xTech, the OSBP event, opening 711 HPW), **not** chasing
> follow-on scaling money that requires an award ATP doesn't have yet. Two immediate implications: (1)
> the pending Navy proposal **still must be disclosed to xTech** — the "substantially the same" rule
> explicitly covers work that is *pending* with another agency, not just awarded work; (2) the Navy
> priority is simply **winning the award** — if it lands as Phase I, the next milestone is the Phase II
> proposal; Navy STP only becomes relevant once ATP holds an active Navy Phase II.
