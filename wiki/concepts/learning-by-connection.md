# Learning by connection

Adults don't learn by memorizing — they learn by **connecting new information to what they
already know.** From [[how-to-remember-everything-brainhealthdecoded]].

- **Children vs adults.** Children start from nothing, so they build knowledge by brute-force
  memorization. Adults already hold rich knowledge networks, so the brain processes new input
  by asking *"what do I already know that relates to this?"* and integrating it. Researchers
  call this **top-down, plausibility-driven processing** — existing knowledge does the heavy
  lifting.
- **The move:** stop trying to *memorize* what you read; try to *connect* it. Let your existing
  frameworks anchor the new idea.
- Debunks the myth that you must "master the foundations first" before doing anything real.

**Why this vault is built this way.** This is the human-cognition mirror of the
[[llm-wiki-pattern|LLM Wiki]] thesis that *the value is in the links, not the pages* — an
ingest's job is to weave new material into what already exists (see [[overview]] and the
AGENTS.md "connection, not accumulation" rule). Reading into this vault *is* learning by
connection. Ties to [[hebbian-learning]] (connections literally wire), [[knowledge-types]]
(concepts specifically demand this), and [[marsh-memory-lab|the Illusion of Explanatory Depth]]
(connecting/explaining exposes the gaps mere familiarity hides).
