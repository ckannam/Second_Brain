# The doorway effect

Why you walk into a room and forget why you came in. The **doorway effect** is the finding
that passing through a doorway makes you **more likely to forget** what you were just holding
in mind — not because of distance or time, but because the doorway acts as an **event
boundary**. It's the concrete mechanism behind the [[conscious-vs-subconscious|tiny working
memory]] getting wiped, and the science backing [[neuro-scripts-batch-1|Neuro Short #1]].

## The finding
- **Gabriel Radvansky's team (Notre Dame, 2011), "Walking through doorways causes forgetting":**
  across experiments in **both real and virtual environments**, subjects forgot more after
  walking through a doorway than after moving the *same distance* within one room. Distance and
  time were controlled — the **doorway itself** was the trigger.
- Later work replicated it and probed the boundary: forgetting shows up at the **event boundary**,
  not from simple interference or fatigue.

## The mechanism — event boundaries
Your mind doesn't store life as one continuous stream; it chunks experience into **events**.
Crossing a doorway signals "this event is over, start a new one" — the brain **updates its
event model** for the new room and **files away** the old context. Whatever you were holding in
the small [[conscious-vs-subconscious|conscious workspace]] ("go get my phone") was tied to the
*old* event model, so it gets dropped as the model refreshes. Same family of idea as
[[predictive-processing|the brain modeling the world]] and why
[[memory-consolidation|context shapes what you recall]] — memory is organized around boundaries.

## Honest caveats (freshness)
- The effect is **real but modest and context-dependent** — it's about *event-model updating*,
  not a literal memory-erase. Competing accounts include perceptual interference and attentional
  lapses at the boundary.
- **Replication is mixed.** More recent studies (incl. 2024–2025 working-memory and
  doorway work) find the effect can be small, can flip, or depends on cognitive load — so present
  it as a well-known, well-motivated phenomenon, **not** a hard law. Don't overclaim in scripts.

## The practical fix
Beat the model-refresh by keeping the goal in the *new* event: **say it out loud, or picture the
object, before you move.** Verbalizing/visualizing re-anchors the intention so it survives the
boundary. (This is what [[neuro-scripts-batch-1|Script 1]] recommends.)

## Why it's in the vault
A clean, universally-relatable [[neuro-channel|Neuro]] Short topic *and* a good illustration of
[[conscious-vs-subconscious|working-memory limits]] in action — the doorway is the everyday proof
that the conscious workspace is tiny and gets overwritten.
