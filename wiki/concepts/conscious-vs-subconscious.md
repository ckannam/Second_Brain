# Conscious vs subconscious mind

A dual-process model of the mind. From [[conscious-subconscious-mind-ixcarus]].

- **Conscious mind** = what you think of as "you": working memory, deliberate reasoning, focus.
  It's tiny — holds ~**7±2** items, processes ~**40–50 bits/sec**, is slow, effortful, and burns
  glucose (why hard thinking is physically tiring). Overlaps [[environment-design|working-memory
  overload]].
- **Subconscious mind** = everything else: filters ~**11 million bits/sec**, runs autonomic
  function, stores all memory, and executes learned behaviors (walking, driving, typing). Fast,
  automatic, pattern-recognition on steroids.
- **Learning is the handoff:** *conscious struggle → subconscious automation*. New/complex input
  gets flagged for conscious attention; once handled, the pattern is stored subconscious for
  automatic reuse next time. This is the same myelination story as [[neuroplasticity]] and the
  [[cue-routine-reward-loop]] "automate the start."

**Consequences worth grounding behavior in:**
- **Emotions are subconscious programs**, many installed in childhood — they fire *before*
  conscious reasoning catches up, which is why you can *know* something yet still *feel*
  otherwise.
- **Reprogramming needs repetition + emotion**, not just knowledge (knowing ≠ rewiring) — the
  affirmation/visualization mechanism, and why it takes thousands of reps. Same lever as
  [[neuroplasticity]].
- The subconscious does much of its integration during sleep → [[memory-consolidation]].

> Framed loosely as Freud's *unconscious* / Jung's *collective unconscious* / modern *implicit
> processing*; the article's "7-second pre-decision" and "superconscious" claims are
> pop-science — treat as motivational framing, not settled science. The durable, well-supported
> core is dual-process cognition (cf. Kahneman System 1/2) and limited working memory.

Its two operational subsystems have their own pages: [[reticular-activating-system]] (the
attention filter) and [[cognitive-biases]] (the shortcuts).
