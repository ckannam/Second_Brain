# How the Duke Innovation Ecosystem Is Actually Set Up

Research answer to Cole's question — the Duke-side detail for the JHU-vs-Duke comparison in the
[[kelli-luginbuhl-call-prep|Kelli conversation]]. **The headline: Duke runs a distributed,
federated ecosystem of strong *separate* units — not a single integrated "front door" the way
[[jhtv|JHTV]] does.** Four pillars coordinate rather than sit inside one pipeline.

## The four pillars

### 1. [[duke-otc|Duke OTC]] — tech transfer (the IP core)
Protects, markets, licenses Duke IP; **New Ventures group** helps faculty spin out companies; runs
SBIR/STTR + I-Corps support. Led by **[[Robin Rasor]]** (AVP; former AUTM president). **FY23: 15
startups, $102M returned** to inventors/university. Framed **licensing-first**.

### 2. [[duke-capital-partners|Duke Capital Partners]] (DCP) — the capital layer
Duke's **early-stage venture arm** — *formerly the Duke Angel Network* (est. 2015), an angel
platform **plus a co-investment fund** (the Duke Innovation Fund) that leverages the **Duke alumni
network**. Evaluates **~1,000 startups/yr**, deep DD on ~200, invests in **12–24**; has deployed
**$105M+ across 60+ companies** (AI, SaaS, health care, med devices; portfolio incl. Otter.ai,
restor3d, Vestwell). A **top-5 university investment entity in North America.** *Key structural
point: Duke's "capital function" is an affiliated angel/fund network, not a staff capital-strategy
team embedded in tech transfer.*

### 3. [[duke-ie|Duke I&E]] — education, culture & space (the pipeline)
The **university-wide Innovation & Entrepreneurship Initiative** — where **[[Kelli Luginbuhl]] &
[[Doug Speight]] teach** (and Cole's **I&E minor**). Physical hubs: **the Bullpen** (13,000 sq ft,
downtown Durham, opened Sept 2015, home base + workspace for 25+ student/alumni startups), **the
Foundry** (7,600 sq ft maker/hardware space), and the **Innovation Co-Lab** (creativity incubator,
3D printing/woodshop). Programs: I&E+ summer, SEAD (social/health entrepreneurship), Melissa & Doug
Entrepreneurs, the Duke Startup Challenge, the I&E certificate.

### 4. [[duke-ctsi-medx|Duke CTSI + MEDx]] — medical translational engine
**CTSI** (Clinical & Translational Science Institute; 100+ faculty; runs Duke's NIH **CTSA**) funds
and resources the path **discovery → preclinical → trials → commercialization**. **MEDx** (since
2015) catalyzes **medicine × engineering** collaborations and feeds early research into CTSI. This
is Duke's health/bio [[valley-of-death]] bridge — housed in the **medical/research enterprise**,
not the ventures office. OTC is a listed CTSI partner.

## The core contrast (the analytical insight for Cole)
| | **JHU / [[jhtv|JHTV]]** | **Duke** |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | **Centralized "front door"** (since 2014) — one integrated org | **Distributed / federated** — 4 strong separate units |
| **What's bundled** | Tech transfer + [[fast-forward|FastForward]] accelerator + [[translational-funding]] + a **dedicated Capital Strategy team** | OTC (licensing/ventures) · DCP (the fund) · I&E (culture/space) · CTSI/MEDx (medical) — coordinated, not merged |
| **Capital function** | **Staff team** that actively maps funders onto each spinout | **Affiliated angel/fund network** (DCP) leveraging alumni |
| **Branding** | **Capital-first** ("front door" to funding) | **Licensing-first** (OTC) + a strong **alumni-angel** engine |
| **A headline metric** | **$4.8B** raised by portfolio (FY25) | **$105M+** deployed by DCP; **$102M** OTC returns (FY23) |

**The whitespace Cole's work points at:** Duke has a deep **alumni-angel capital network** and
strong **maker/education culture**, but *no single capital-first "front door"* that systematically
maps the right funders onto each spinout the way JHTV's [[capital-strategy|Capital Strategy]] team —
and Cole's **[[vc-matching-second-brain|matcher]]** — do. That's the exact gap to raise with Kelli:
*would a JHTV-style capital-strategy layer strengthen Duke's federated model?*
