# Neuroanatomy landmarks (for fMRI)

Working knowledge of gross brain anatomy needed to localize and describe fMRI activations. From the
[[fmri-lab1-neuroanatomy|Week 2 neuroanatomy lab]] ("Lab: Intro to Neuroanatomy"). Complements the
systems-level anatomy in [[neurosci-206]] (esp. its Wk1 note [[neurosci-206-human-neuroanatomy]]).

## Why anatomy at all
- The brain (~**1.3 kg, ~100 billion neurons**) is studied by MRI at the scale of **millimeters** —
  MRI resolves **gross anatomy**, not individual neurons (which are ~tens of micrometers, far below
  MRI resolution). See spatial resolution in [[mri-contrast#Resolution]].
- You must be able to **consistently identify regions** to build/test brain-based hypotheses, read
  the literature, and talk with other investigators.

## Viewing convention (SPM)
- 3-D volumes shown in **three orthogonal planes**: **Coronal, Sagittal, Axial** (clockwise from
  left in [[spm|SPM]]'s Display). Coordinates read out in **voxel (vx)** and **millimeter (mm)** space.

## Landmarks to be able to locate
Amygdala · **Anterior commissure / Posterior commissure (AC–PC)** · Pons · Inferior frontal gyrus ·
Anterior cingulate gyrus · Hippocampus · **Central sulcus** (separates motor/somatosensory) ·
**Inferior colliculus** (subcortical auditory station) · **Insular cortex** · **First transverse
gyrus of Heschl** (primary auditory cortex). Functional landmarks used in lab: **motor cortex**
(finger-tapping activation) and **visual cortex**.
