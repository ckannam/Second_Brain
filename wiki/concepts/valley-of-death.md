# The Valley of Death (tech commercialization)

The **funding-and-development gap between a lab discovery and a market-ready product** — the
stage where a technology is too mature for basic-research grants but too unproven for private
investors, so it stalls and often dies for lack of capital. The single concept Cole flagged as
**most useful** from [[Kelli Luginbuhl]] & [[Doug Speight]]'s course
[[business-of-science-syllabus-spring2026|The Business of Science]] (I&E 710).

## Why it's the load-bearing idea
It reframes the whole commercialization problem: **the bottleneck for academic science usually
isn't the invention — it's surviving the valley.** Everything else in the ecosystem debate hangs
off this:
- **[[translational-funding|Translational / non-dilutive funding]]** exists *specifically to
  bridge the valley of death* — de-risking the science (proof-of-concept, prototype, early
  validation) until it's investable. SBIR/STTR, MII/TEDCO, MSCRF, and JHTV's Cohen/Thalheimer/Zizic
  funds are all valley-of-death bridges.
- **[[capital-strategy|JHTV's Capital Strategy]]** function is, in effect, a *valley-of-death
  navigation team* — mapping which funder can carry a spinout across each stretch.
- Cole's **[[vc-matching-second-brain|VC-matcher]]** and **[[jhtv-grant-finder|grant finder]]**
  are tools for finding the exact capital that gets a technology across the valley.

## The JHU vs. Duke lens (for the [[kelli-luginbuhl-call-prep|Kelli conversation]])
The sharpest comparison question: *which ecosystem has built the better bridge across the valley
of death?* JHU/JHTV has an unusually deep, staged non-dilutive escalator plus a dedicated capital
team. Good question for Kelli: how does Duke (via [[Duke OTC]] / SBIR-STTR / the PhD Innovators
Program) get its spinouts — like her own [[Isolere Bio]] — across the same gap?
