# ATP positioning — "readiness, not medicine"

The single strategic rule that governs everything [[athletic-training-portal|ATP]] says about
itself: **position the product as commander-/coach-facing *readiness* and *training-load*
monitoring — a flag on *deviation from an individual's own baseline* — never as a clinical device
that diagnoses, predicts, or treats injury.** This page consolidates a thread that until now lived
only as asides across the ATP cluster ([[athletic-training-portal]], [[atp-competitive-analysis]],
[[atp-federal-funding-landscape]], the [[atp-dod-federal-landscape-2026|Oct 2026 source memo]]).
It is the *why* behind the wording in every white paper, pitch, and funding application.

## The two lines the framing stays on the right side of

ATP's framing is not marketing gloss — it is simultaneously dodging **two different exclusion
lines**, and the same sentence has to clear both:

1. **The FDA device line (the [[WHOOP]] warning-letter trap).** A claim to *predict or detect
   injury/illness* turns a wellness product into an unapproved medical **device**. Per
   [[atp-competitive-analysis]] §5, the FDA issued WHOOP a warning letter (Jul 14, 2025) over exactly
   that kind of claim; WHOOP survived only by retreating to General Wellness language after the FDA's
   Jan 6, 2026 guidance update. ATP's "predict injuries before they happen" instinct is the same trap
   → the de-risked form is **"deviation-from-baseline monitoring"** (a general-wellness-safe signal),
   not a prediction.
2. **The Army/DoD medical-command exclusion.** On the federal side
   ([[atp-federal-funding-landscape]]), anything that reads as *medicine* gets routed to medical
   command and out of the readiness/training programs ATP is actually trying to sell into. Framing
   ATP as **unit-readiness / training-load / availability for commanders** keeps it in the
   human-performance lane (711 HPW, AFWERX readiness, SOCOM human performance) rather than the
   clinical-research lane (MTEC/CDMRP), where ATP has no clinical partner or trial data.

The elegant part: **both lines want the same move.** "Flag a deviation from this person's own
baseline, for the person's coach/commander to act on" is simultaneously (a) general-wellness-safe
for the FDA and (b) readiness-not-medicine for DoD. One reframe clears both exclusions.

## How it threads through each surface

- **Product claims** — replace any "predicts/prevents injury" language with baseline-deviation and
  availability/readiness language. (Entity page [[athletic-training-portal]] §Competitive landscape:
  "reframe to wellness-safe deviation-from-baseline monitoring.")
- **Competitive wedge** — the FDA-cleared incumbent **[[DARI Motion]]** *owns* the medical-device
  lane; ATP cannot win there and should not try. ATP's defensible wedge is the wellness/readiness
  layer DARI's clearance does **not** cover — so the positioning is also the competitive strategy,
  not just compliance ([[atp-competitive-analysis]]).
- **Federal framing** — every DoD artifact (the 711 HPW CHEERS white paper, an AFWERX readiness FOT,
  the [[xtech-search|xTech]] pitch, the SOCOM Biomedical & Human Performance BAA) leads with
  commander-facing readiness. The Navy NV068 work is already framed as an **"Aircrew Readiness
  Fingerprint,"** which is the template ([[atp-federal-funding-landscape]]).
- **State/Duke framing** — less regulatory-sensitive, but consistency matters: the non-dilutive
  applications ([[atp-nc-nondilutive-funding]]) should carry the same readiness language so the story
  is identical whoever reads it.

## Why it's a concept page, not a task

This is a **durable positioning principle**, not a dated action — it does not go stale the way the
funding deadlines do. When a new funding vehicle or competitor appears, the question "how does ATP
describe itself here?" has one answer, and it lives here. Link to this page from any ATP pitch,
white-paper draft, or funding application as the positioning check.
