# Environment design

Strategy #4 for [[discipline-without-willpower]]: shape your surroundings so the
disciplined action is the low-friction default and distractions are removed.

Two levers ([[erin-meryl]]):
- **Reduce clutter.** A cluttered environment overloads working memory (which holds few
  items at once), making focus hard. Keep the workspace clear.
- **Reduce friction to the goal.** Do interim steps in advance: lay out running shoes and
  clothes the night before, pull up the essay materials before bed. This is also why
  writing goals the night before helps.

Sits alongside [[cue-routine-reward-loop]] (cues live in the environment) and is a direct
counter to [[temporal-discounting]] by lowering the present cost of starting. Broadly this
is a "nudge" approach (Thaler & Sunstein 2008).

**Worked example — beating the scroll ([[doomscrolling-attention-science]]):** the reason "I'll only
scroll 15 minutes" fails is that it's a *time* limit fighting willpower. The environment-design version is
a **context limit** — bind the behavior to a place/activity ("I only scroll on the treadmill / while
walking") so it's structurally unavailable the rest of the day. Same family as "move the monkey out of the
room": change the surroundings, not the resolve.

**Neuro grounding** ([[neuroplasticity-rewiring-brain-ixcarus]]): environmental cues keep the
old [[neuroplasticity|pathway]] firing (why quitting smoking around smokers fails), and
**mirror neurons** mean you literally rehearse the habits of people you're around — "show me
your 5 closest friends." So the highest-leverage move is to change your environment (and social
environment) to one where the target behavior is the low-friction, *normal* default. See
[[neuroscience-of-behavior]].

Source: [[discipline-without-willpower-erin-meryl]].
