# Memory consolidation

Learning has two stages: **consumption** (encoding new information) and **digestion**
(consolidating it into long-term memory). The second is the one everyone skips. From
[[how-to-remember-everything-brainhealthdecoded]], with the sleep mechanism corroborated by
[[neuroplasticity-rewiring-brain-ixcarus]].

## The mechanism

- New information creates a **fragile hippocampal trace** — "like dragging a stick through wet
  sand." For it to become a durable memory the brain needs a **consolidation window**: rest in
  which it replays the trace, strengthens the connections, and integrates it into long-term
  cortical networks.
- Consuming without digesting is "writing paragraph after paragraph in wet sand" — the waves
  wash each one away. Double-speed podcasts and half-attention TED talks optimize consumption
  and skip digestion, which is why almost no insight survives.
- **Consolidation happens mostly during sleep.** [[neuroplasticity|Plasticity]] created during
  effort doesn't lock in until sleep replays it — so chronic sleep deprivation means you
  "start from scratch" the next day no matter how hard you worked.
- **The molecular clock (why spacing works).** Consolidation runs on slow signaling cascades —
  ERK/PKA and **CREB-driven transcription** — that take *time* to strengthen synapses. Spacing
  sessions lets this machinery finish; massing them too close can recruit *inhibitory* regulators
  that suppress consolidation. This gives the [[spacing-effect]] a mechanistic basis, and means the
  best inter-session gap is a computable, intermediate optimum
  ([[optimizing-spaced-learning-smolen-2016]], [[neuroscience-of-spacing-brainfacts]]).

## What to do

- **Process before you move on** — the non-negotiable step. Hit a skill? Go attempt it. A
  concept you can't explain? Explain it out loud until you can. A fact you'll need? Make the
  flashcard *now*. You cover less material but actually keep it.
- **Space it out.** "20 minutes daily beats 2 hours on Saturday" — five sessions give the brain
  five nights of consolidation; one gives it one.
- **Protect sleep** as part of any learning or habit project.
- Sleep does double duty: deep NREM both consolidates learning *and* runs the
  [[glymphatic-system|brain's waste-clearance drain]] — which is why [[alcohol-sleep-dementia|alcohol's]]
  hit on deep sleep costs you memory *and* long-run brain health.
