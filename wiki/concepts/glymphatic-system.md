# Glymphatic system — the brain's overnight waste-clearance plumbing

The brain has no conventional lymphatic vessels, so it clears metabolic waste through a
glia-dependent pathway named the **glymphatic system** (glial + lymphatic). Cerebrospinal
fluid (CSF) is driven along the outside of arteries into the brain, flushes through the
**interstitial space**, and carries soluble waste out along veins to be dumped into the true
lymphatics and blood. It is the drain that keeps neural tissue clean. This is the mechanistic
linchpin connecting [[memory-consolidation|sleep]] to long-term brain health, so it anchors the
[[neuroscience-of-behavior]] cluster's "why sleep is non-negotiable" story.

## The mechanism (why it runs at night)
- **Slow-wave (deep NREM) sleep is when the drain opens.** In the landmark mouse work (Xie et
  al., *Science* 2013, "Sleep drives metabolite clearance from the adult brain"), the
  interstitial space **expanded ~60% during sleep**, sharply lowering resistance to fluid flow
  and roughly doubling the rate of waste clearance vs. waking.
- **Norepinephrine is the switch.** During deep sleep, the noradrenergic arousal signal drops;
  that collapse is what lets the interstitial space open and CSF flush through. Awake, high-NE
  "conscious computation" mode keeps the drain mostly closed — the brain can't fully think and
  fully clean at the same time.
- **Astrocytes are the pipes.** Flow depends on **aquaporin-4 (AQP4)** water channels correctly
  positioned on astrocyte endfeet wrapping the vasculature. Mislocalize AQP4 and clearance
  fails even if you're asleep.

## Why it matters for dementia
- The waste it clears includes **amyloid-β (Aβ) and tau** — the two proteins that aggregate in
  Alzheimer's disease. Less clearance (or more production) tips the balance toward plaques and
  tangles.
- **Human validation:** a randomized crossover trial showed that a **normal night's sleep
  raised morning plasma Aβ and tau** vs. sleep deprivation — i.e., sleep physically *exports*
  these biomarkers from brain to blood ([Nature Communications 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-68374-8)).
- Nedergaard & Goldman framed **"glymphatic failure as a final common pathway to dementia"**
  ([*Science* 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abb8739)): aging, poor sleep,
  and vascular damage all converge on a failing drain.

## What breaks it
- **Sleep loss / fragmented sleep** — less slow-wave sleep = less clearance time.
- **Aging** — glymphatic flow and AQP4 polarization decline with age.
- **Heavy alcohol** — suppresses both deep sleep *and* the astrocyte plumbing directly. See
  [[alcohol-sleep-dementia]] for the full chain and evidence.
