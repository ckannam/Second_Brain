# Predictive processing

The brain constantly generates predictions about what happens next based on what usually
happens, then acts to make those predictions come true. From
[[neuroplasticity-rewiring-brain-ixcarus]] (and the filtering half from
[[conscious-subconscious-mind-ixcarus]]).

- If you usually procrastinate, the brain predicts procrastination today and generates the
  resistance-to-starting that fulfills it. If you usually eat badly, it generates the
  cravings. It runs a **self-fulfilling prophecy loop keyed to your past behavior.**
- So changing a habit isn't just fighting the habit — you're fighting the brain's entire
  **predictive model of who you are.** This is why [[identity-led-goals|identity-level change]]
  is more powerful than behavior-level change: identity is the model's ground truth.
- The model updates on **behavioral evidence, not statements.** "I'm disciplined" is dismissed
  as noise if behavior disagrees; accumulated action is what shifts the prediction. This is the
  mechanistic root of "fake it till the evidence makes it real."

**Filtering side — the [[reticular-activating-system]].** Predictive processing also decides
what you *perceive*: the brain surfaces evidence that matches its predictions (you buy a car,
suddenly see it everywhere) and filters out the rest. Directed consciously, this is
programmable; left on autopilot it becomes [[cognitive-biases|confirmation bias]].

Connects the whole behavior-change cluster: [[neuroplasticity]], [[discipline-without-willpower]],
[[temporal-discounting]].
