# Cold email + hidden-role sourcing (the job-search playbook)

Synthesis of a tight cluster of job-search sources Cole clipped 2026-07-29 (all from the **Ben Lang /
Next Play** ecosystem). The reusable methodology behind the [[outreach-pipeline]] engine; the concrete
rules here are the **drafting + sourcing standard** the [[outreach-kit]] enforces.

## Why this matters
The best roles at breakout startups are **filled before they're ever posted** — through relationships,
not job boards. So the winning move is: **spot a company early → reach the founder/hiring manager
directly with a short, specific message, before the role hits a board.** Discovery + warm-specific
outreach beats mass applications. (This is exactly Cole's [[cole-job-search-strategy|relationship-first
strategy]].)

## Part 1 — the cold-email rules ("make it 99% less bad")
Source: [Next Play — "The guide to getting a job with cold email"](https://nextplayso.substack.com/p/the-guide-to-getting-a-job-with-cold). What **not** to do (the pre-send checklist):
- **≤ ~200 words.** No life story. Less is more.
- **No fancy words / buzzwords.** Reduce "executed on key initiatives" → "drove impact" → "helped grow
  ARR $1M in 3 months." Read it aloud; does it sound like a human?
- **Always include one specific ask.** Never make the reader guess the next step.
- **Never be vague.** Replace abstractions with examples ("startups like X and Y").
- **No spam, no dishonesty, no *fake* personalization.** Don't fake "been a fan for years." It's fine to
  say you found them because they just raised — be a real, honest human. Max 1–2 follow-ups.

**Mindset:** you can email *any* company even with no posted role, no matching city, no "required"
degree — startups make exceptions. Cold email works best when you're **genuinely competent at something
valuable and have shown it.**

## Part 2 — the format (3 sentences)
1. **Who you are.** 2. **Why you're reaching out.** 3. **Why they should care.**
That's it. The famous examples (Niraj Pant → Evan Spiegel; the copywriter → founder) are short, plain,
and specific — anyone could write them; they just avoid the mistakes above. Volume matters too: expect
to email **~10–30** companies; luck + timing mean even great emails often go unanswered.

## Part 3 — where to find the unposted roles (9 sources)
Source: [Ben Lang, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/benmlang). Feed these into [[outreach-pipeline]] discovery:
1. **Ramp vendor reports** — who fast-growing companies spend on ([ramp.com/data](https://ramp.com/data))
2. **Harmonic Hot 25** — ranked fastest-growing startups ([harmonic.ai/hot-25-startups](https://harmonic.ai/hot-25-startups))
3. **VC portfolio job boards** — Sequoia / a16z / Index / Greylock
4. **Founders You Should Know** ([foundersysk.com](https://foundersysk.com))
5. **Next Play** newsletter — under-the-radar startups + roles
6. **YC startup directory** ([ycombinator.com/jobs](https://www.ycombinator.com/jobs))
7. **Early Days** Substack
8. **a16z Build** newsletter
9. **HN "Who's Hiring"** monthly threads (no recruiters allowed)

> The sharpest comment on the post (Milana Raiter): these sources solve **discovery**; the roles filled
> before posting are filled through **relationships**. Combine both — use the signal to spot a company
> early, then send a warm, specific message to a founder/hiring manager. **The list gets you the target;
> the message gets you the conversation.**
