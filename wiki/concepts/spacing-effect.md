# The spacing effect (distributed practice)

Spreading practice across **multiple sessions over time** produces stronger, longer-lasting memory
than the same amount of practice **massed** into one session. One of the most robust findings in
learning science — observed across domains, ages, and even species. It is the phenomenon that
[[spaced-repetition]] tools operationalize, and the mechanistic backbone of the
[[neuroscience-of-behavior]] cluster.

## How robust — the numbers
- **Domain-general:** g ≈ 0.46 (Donovan & Radosevich 1999); up to g = 0.80 for second-language
  learning (Kim & Webb 2022).
- **Mathematics:** a small-to-medium but **robust** effect, g = 0.28 (27 studies, 53 effect sizes)
  — from [[spacing-math-meta-analysis-murray-2025]]. Larger for **isolated** material (g = 0.43)
  than **course-embedded** (g = 0.24): the more a topic is mixed into an ongoing course, the more
  the pure spacing signal is diluted by [[interleaving]] and review.
- **Not just facts — skills too:** combined spacing + testing preserved *Basic Life Support skill*
  retention at 3 months where a single-session control decayed
  ([[spacing-testing-complex-skills-study]]). Spacing protects [[knowledge-types|procedural
  skill]], not only isolated facts.

## Why it works — mechanism (Cole's angle)
- **Consolidation windows.** Each spaced session lands after the prior trace has begun
  consolidating, so the brain reinforces an *existing* trace rather than overwriting a fresh one.
  The [[memory-consolidation|consume-vs-digest]] story, applied to scheduling.
- **Molecular time constants.** Spaced training engages CREB-driven transcription and ERK/PKA
  signaling cascades that take *time* to act; massing sessions too close together can recruit
  *inhibitory* regulators that suppress consolidation. Spacing lets the slow molecular machinery
  finish — corroborated in fruit flies, sea slugs, and rodents
  ([[neuroscience-of-spacing-brainfacts]]).
- **The optimal gap is an intermediate, computable optimum** — not "longer is always better."
  Because it's set by these molecular kinetics, computational models can *predict* the best
  (often irregular, non-uniform) schedule, which can outperform fixed intervals
  ([[optimizing-spaced-learning-smolen-2016]]; extended from sea slugs to a **mammal** in
  [[enhancing-learning-rats-computational-protocol-2023|rats, 2023]]). This is the scientific basis
  for a *smarter-than-generic scheduler* — the [[neuro-channel|learning-app]] thesis. *(Inference:
  model-system optima don't translate turnkey to classroom scheduling — FSRS already approximates
  human forgetting curves empirically.)*

## The catch
Everyone knows spacing works and **almost no one does it** — it's "a slower, harder way to
remember" and cramming *feels* more productive ([[neuroscience-of-spacing-brainfacts]]). The
behavior gap — not the algorithm — is the real problem to solve. Pairs with
[[retrieval-practice]], [[interleaving]], and [[knowledge-types]].
