# Study Playbook — the 3-phase pass for any new topic

Cole's repeatable method for learning a new block of course material (built while prepping
[[chem-210]] Midterm 1, but course-agnostic). Run the three phases **in order** for each topic;
don't jump to problems before the ideas are solid. Pairs with the per-topic knowledge notes in
`coursework/` — those hold the durable facts; this note holds the *process*.

## Phase 1 — Main ideas (ELI15)
Before any slide or equation, get the **big picture in plain language**, explained as if to a
smart 15-year-old. Goal: a mental "table of contents" and the *why* behind the topic.
- 4–7 core ideas, each one or two sentences, no jargon (or jargon immediately unpacked).
- Answer: *What is this topic really about? Why does it matter? How do the pieces connect?*
- Cole should be able to re-tell the story out loud before moving on. This is the checkpoint.

## Phase 2 — Walk the slides
Go through the actual lecture slides **in order** and make sure nothing is a black box.
- For each slide/section: what is it showing, what's the takeaway, how does it map to a Phase-1 idea?
- Surface every term, symbol, and equation — define it, don't skim past it.
- Flag the slides Cox marks as examples / self-tests → those become Phase-3 problems.
- Source of truth for CHEM 210 Unit 1 slides: [[chem-210-unit1-nuclear-quantum]].

## Phase 3 — Work problems
Now build the muscle. Drill the problem types the slides flag as exam templates.
- Do problems actively (Cole solves, then we check), not by reading solutions.
- Prioritize the "likely exam template" problems the knowledge notes call out.
- A topic is "done" when Cole can hit every item in that note's **Learning outcomes to self-check**.

## Why this order
Ideas first gives a scaffold to *hang* slide details on; slides make the ideas concrete and
expose exam-flagged examples; problems convert understanding into recall speed under a
closed-book, no-Wi-Fi calculator exam. Skipping Phase 1 is the classic trap — you memorize
procedures without the concept and can't adapt when a problem is phrased differently.
