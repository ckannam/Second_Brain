# Tech / phone addiction

The claim ([[arthur-brooks]], [[phone-addiction-flow-arthur-brooks]]) that phone use is a **literal
addiction** — same neurochemistry, roughly the same mechanism, as drugs or alcohol — that hides in plain
sight because *everyone* has it ("ask a fish how's the water"). This is the **mechanism + protocol**
page; the **attention-science evidence** on short-form video lives in its sibling
[[doomscrolling-attention-science]] (they cover different angles of the same problem — cross-read them).

## The mechanism
- Dopamine is **anticipation of reward**, not pleasure (see [[temporal-discounting]]). It evolved to
  make us *seek* (berries, mates, food). Notifications, feeds, and slot machines **artificially juice**
  that seeking circuit — "your brain is working the way it's supposed to, but it's been hijacked."
- **Brooks's addiction test:** put the phone away for an hour; if you think of it or reach for it **more
  than once every 5 minutes, you're an addict.** (Habituation, not a moral failing — but real.)
- **Numbing device.** We're evolved for 4 negative emotions (anger, disgust, sadness, fear) — ~16% of a
  normal day. The healthy handling is relationships / apology / helping others; the phone instead
  **numbs** them like whiskey does. "Vegging out" on conflict-and-comparison feeds actually **raises**
  anxiety while feeling like rest, and re-fuels the cycle → short-term relief, long-term escalation
  (the alcohol-rebound parallel).

## Recovery (3 stages, from the addiction literature)
1. **Get mad** — hit "rock bottom" = decide you've had it; the spirit of the rebel.
2. **Detox** — treat it as biology; deliberately interrupt the brain's addicted cycles.
3. **Live differently** — change your circumstances and become comfortable **alone with yourself and
   bored.** The thing you're really escaping is *you* — Brooks's Emersonian path: **be honest with
   yourself → think for yourself → go your own way.**

## Prevention protocol (the actionable part)
This is [[environment-design]] applied to the phone — structure beats willpower.
- **Tech-free *times*:** the **first hour** of the day (sets the tone; ignites the **default-mode
  network**, like shower-thoughts), **meal times** (a face-down phone interrupts **oxytocin** bonding),
  and the **last hour** before bed (blue light → melatonin → sleep architecture).
- **Tech-free *zones*:** the **bedroom** (a $5 alarm clock replaces the phone; after ~6–7 weeks you
  won't want it there) and the **classroom**.
- **Tech *fasts*:** **~96 hours/year** (4 days) fully offline.
- **Change the odds:** **notifications off** (keep one — mom); **grayscale the screen** (the occipital
  lobe is wired for color, so black-and-white makes the phone measurably less magnetic).
