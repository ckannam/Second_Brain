# VC Interview Prep (Bessemer's "How to prepare" framework)

Bessemer Venture Partners' official prep guidance for VC interviews, from their recruiting deck
(source `raw/Processed/bessemer-how-to-prepare-slide.png`, captured 2026-09-15). This is the
**study plan Cole is using to get better across all his VC interviews** ([[vc-investor-applications]],
[[dorm room fund]], Bessemer, Contrary). Four pillars.

## 1. Get to know the specific firm
- Read the firm's own writing (for Bessemer, the **Bessemer Atlas Blog**) to see what they are up to.
- Follow the firm and its investors on X / Twitter (e.g. @BessemerVP).
- Listen to the firm's podcast (Bessemer's is **Wish I Knew**).
> Apply per firm. Before a [[dorm room fund]] interview, read DRF's writing + partners; before Bessemer, read Atlas.

## 2. Stay informed about tech
- Subscribe to newsletters like **TechCrunch** and **Term Sheet** (Fortune).
- Listen to tech / startup podcasts like **How I Built This** and **The Twenty Minute VC**.
- **Take note of interesting early stage companies you hear about** (build a running list, this is also sourcing practice, see [[deal-sourcing]]).

## 3. Practice asking questions
- Think about **what makes a company successful.**
- Get familiar with **business strategies and pricing models.**
- For any company, ask **what questions you would have about how it operates.**
> This is the muscle they test live. Practice on Cole's own examples, [[everyday-robotics]] and [[athletic-training-portal]].

## 4. Get excited about a category
- **Find something that interests you and develop real opinions about it.**
- Read deep category blogs like **Wait But Why**, **Stratechery** (Ben Thompson), and **Matthew Ball**.
> Cole's category is locked, **techbio / AI drug discovery** (see [[vc-investor-applications]] and the two-school pairing: JHU techbio, Duke defense/dual-use via [[athletic-training-portal]]). Develop a written point of view on it.
