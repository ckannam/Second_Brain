# Spacing & retrieval practice for math — meta-analysis (Murray et al. 2025)

A pre-registered meta-analysis (University of York / Oslo) asking whether the [[spacing-effect]]
and [[retrieval-practice|testing effect]] — powerhouse findings in verbal learning — actually
transfer to **mathematics**. The keystone evidence source for the vault's spaced-learning cluster.
PDF preserved at `raw/Processed/Murrayetal_EdPsychReview_2025.pdf`.

## Headline findings
- **Spacing works for math (robust):** spaced vs massed g = **0.28** (27 studies, 53 effect
  sizes). Larger but less robust for **isolated** material (g = 0.43) than **course-embedded**
  (g = 0.24) — embedding dilutes the pure spacing signal with review/[[interleaving]].
- **Retrieval practice for math is *not* robust:** testing vs restudy g = **0.18**, 95% CI
  **crosses zero** (7 studies, 32 effect sizes). Contrast with g ≈ 0.50 for retrieval in general
  domains — a genuine domain caveat, not a universal law.
- **Interleaving** (adjacent literature they cite): g = 0.34 for math (Brunmair & Richter 2019).
- **Context:** general spacing g ≈ 0.46 (Donovan & Radosevich 1999); L2 language up to g = 0.80
  (Kim & Webb 2022).

## Why it matters for Cole
This is the evidence that **kills the naive "turn everything into flashcards" model** and justifies
knowledge-type routing: facts → cards; skills/problem-solving → spaced [[interleaving|interleaved
practice]], because flashcard-style retrieval underperforms in math. Directly shapes the
[[knowledge-types]] strategy table and the [[neuro-channel|learning-app]] thesis. Ties to the
[[marsh-memory-lab|metacognition]] angle and [[spacing-effect]] / [[retrieval-practice]].

**Freshness:** point-in-time snapshot of the math-education literature (small # of retrieval
studies is the stated limitation — absence of a robust effect ≠ evidence of no effect).
