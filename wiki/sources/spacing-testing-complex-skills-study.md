# Combined spacing + testing for complex clinical skills (quasi-experimental)

A nursing-education study testing whether **combined [[spacing-effect|spacing]] +
[[retrieval-practice|testing]]** improves retention of **complex clinical skills** (Basic Life
Support / Advanced Life Support simulation) — a domain where retention notoriously decays.

## Findings
- **Intervention** (combined spacing + testing, n = 13) vs **control** (single-session training,
  no testing, n = 18), measured at **3 months**:
  - **BLS:** intervention retained knowledge **and** skills (no significant drop); control decayed
    significantly on both.
  - **ALS:** more mixed — some decay in the intervention group on ALS knowledge; ALS skills held in
    both. (Small sample; a quasi-experiment, not an RCT.)
- **Claim:** first study to show combined spacing + testing can be "highly effective for complex
  skills simulation training" at 3 months.

## Why it matters
This is the evidence that **spacing/retrieval protect *skills*, not just facts** — countering the
tempting read that the [[knowledge-types|Skill bucket]] is outside spaced-learning's reach. Supports
scheduling spaced, [[interleaving|interleaved]] *practice* (perform-the-procedure retrieval) for
skills. Feeds [[spacing-effect]] and the [[neuro-channel|learning-app]] Skill-bucket design.

**Caveat:** small n and mixed ALS results — suggestive, not definitive.
