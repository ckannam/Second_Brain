# Source: Huettel Ch. 2 — MRI Scanners

Chapter 2 of the [[huettel-fmri-textbook|course textbook]] (Week 1 reading). Exam-oriented digest;
full OCR notes captured. Feeds the scanner/safety material in [[mri-physics]].

## Six components (mnemonic M-R-I + 3 support)
- **M — Static magnetic field:** generated by **superconducting electromagnets** (niobium-titanium
  alloy) cooled to **~12 K (−261 °C)** with **liquid-helium cryogens** → strong, stable field, no
  power loss. Two demands: **homogeneity** (uniform field; else image distortion; **Helmholtz pair**
  design) and **field strength** (human fMRI **1.5–11 T**; small-animal >20 T; unit **tesla**, after
  Nikola Tesla). History: lodestones (ancient China) → Ørsted (1820, current deflects compass).
- **R — Radiofrequency coils:** **excitation** (RF pulse perturbs nuclei) + **reception** (detect
  re-emitted energy = raw MR signal). Types: **surface** (high local sensitivity, poor depth),
  **volume** (uniform), **phased array** (best of both → **parallel imaging**). **Q = (1/R)√(L/C)**.
- **I — Gradient coils:** superimpose linear spatial gradients → **image formation / spatial
  encoding**; turned on briefly. **Maxwell pair** = z-gradient (parallel to B₀); **Golay/double-saddle**
  = x/y (perpendicular). Criteria: linearity & strength (~10–30 mT/m); power ∝ ~5th power of bore.
- **Shimming coils** (correct residual field inhomogeneity, adjusted per subject, ~0.1 ppm),
  **computer hardware/software** (pulse-sequence control + reconstruction), **experimental control**
  (stimulus presentation via MR-safe goggles, response via button box/joystick, synchronization) and
  **physiological monitoring** (heart rate, respiration, CO₂, skin conductance, pupil/eye position).

## Box 2.1 — Outline of an fMRI experiment
IRB approval & recruitment → **metal screening** (the BIAC-style screening form) → set up subject
(earplugs, goggles, volume coil, squeeze-ball panic device) → **structural scan first (~3–5 min,
quiet), then functional (~40 min, loud)** → debrief. Mirrors what Cole's group will do at [[biac|BIAC]].

## MRI safety — three domains (exam-critical)
1. **Static field → projectile effect** (greatest risk): **translation** (pulled along an axis) &
   **torsion** (twists to align with field). **Cardinal rule: no ferromagnetic metal in the scanner
   room.** Ferromagnetic = iron, nickel, cobalt, rare-earths (gadolinium, dysprosium); safe = plastic,
   tin, **titanium**, lead. Fatal 2001 oxygen-canister death of a 6-year-old; 1992 aneurysm-clip death.
2. **Gradient field → dB/dt:** **peripheral nerve stimulation** (tingling/twitch); don't clasp
   hands/cross legs (conductive loops). Risk to pacemakers (MR-conditional devices exist since ~2011).
3. **RF field → heating (SAR, W/kg):** non-ionizing but deposits heat; **larger flip angles (180°>90°)**
   and more pulses/time raise SAR; rises with field strength. Looped wires (EKG leads) can burn.
- Plus **claustrophobia** (~10% clinical, 1–3% research; mock scanner helps) and **acoustic noise**
  (>95 dB, 1000–4000 Hz, from **Lorentz forces** on gradient wires → ear protection mandatory).
- Field effects on physiology are minimal: transient **phosphenes**/metallic taste on fast head
  movement; magnetohydrodynamic effect negligible (~18 T to shift BP by 1 mmHg). Erhard et al. 1995
  (4 T): 45% reported sensations **with the magnet powered but no field present** (suggestion).
