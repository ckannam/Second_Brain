## Summary

Central thesis: [[discipline-without-willpower|discipline has almost nothing to do with
willpower]]. Willpower is a prefrontal-cortex function — cognitively expensive, degrades
under stress/fatigue, and a poor foundation for lasting change. The most disciplined
people don't out-fight impulses; they restructure brain and environment so the fight
never happens.

Over 90% of why discipline feels hard: **cost is in the present, reward is in the
future** — the brain's dopamine system steeply discounts future rewards
([[temporal-discounting]]). "Think about your long-term goals" fails because it asks
willpower to override hardwired biology.

Five neuroscience-backed strategies:
1. [[temptation-bundling]] — pair a hard task with an immediate reward; break tasks into
   small units for dopamine hits on completion.
2. [[cue-routine-reward-loop|Automate via cue-routine-reward]] — condition a cue so the
   hard task starts with less friction.
3. [[identity-led-goals]] — "I am someone who trains" beats "I want to go to the gym."
4. [[environment-design]] — reduce friction and clutter; lay out cues in advance.
5. Understand the neuroscience (the point of the video).

## Research referenced

Milkman et al. 2014 (temptation bundling), Amabile & Kramer 2011 (Progress Principle),
Graybiel 2008 (habits), Lally et al. 2010 (66-day habit study), Brooks 2014 (reappraising
anxiety), Thaler & Sunstein 2008 (Nudge).
