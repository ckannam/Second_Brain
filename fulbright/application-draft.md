### 3. Abstract / Summary of Proposal *(1750 char max, no links — 1,674)*

> For most of history, founding a company demanded three scarce resources: capital to build before earning revenue, expertise across engineering, design, law, and market research, and infrastructure to reach customers. Where those were hardest to assemble, most would-be founders simply could not start. Artificial intelligence is now collapsing all three barriers at once: a single founder can prototype software, draft contracts, run market research, and reach customers with tools that cost almost nothing.
>
> My project asks where, and for whom, that unlock matters most, and how it is reshaping who gets to become an entrepreneur in Brazil's emerging economy. I will base the work in Recife, in the Northeast, where a fast-growing digital economy anchored by CESAR and the Porto Digital ecosystem is developing outside the traditional São Paulo–Rio axis, an ideal setting to study entrepreneurship in a historically underserved region.
>
> Over nine months, affiliated with CESAR, I will: (1) embed with founders, incubatees, and faculty to observe how early-stage ventures use AI day to day; (2) interview founders, investors, and support-organization leaders on which barrier, capital, expertise, or infrastructure, AI relaxes most, and what new bottlenecks appear; and (3) build case studies of ventures that could not plausibly have started five years ago, tracing exactly which AI capabilities made them viable. I will synthesize the findings into a written analysis shared back with the host community.
>
> I bring a practitioner's lens: I work at Johns Hopkins Technology Ventures on the venture-capital and translational-funding team, and I co-founded a student venture.

### Flexibility & Adaptability *(2000 max — 1,676 chars)*
Flagship story: the University Shipping overloaded-truck crisis night ([[uship]]). *(Revised 2026-08-20: removed "rich Duke kid" contrast from the Baltimore line.)*

> I adapt best by treating unfamiliarity as information to gather, not a threat to manage — a habit built from running a company that only works when the plan meets reality.
>
> I co-own University Shipping, a six-figure student moving company. On the third night of a cross-country run, both trucks were packed, we had twenty-plus pickups the next morning, it was 11:30 PM, we had worked sixteen hours, and every rental yard was closed. Our standard plan simply failed. Panicking or forcing the old approach would have sunk us. Instead we stopped, called every customer for an exact item count, and rebuilt the load plan around real numbers — I finished the drive with boxes in the cab and a side mirror resting on my head. Two lessons stuck: build a fail-safe into anything that matters, and in a crisis the hard part is rarely the physical problem but aligning tired people on one goal with good information.
>
> That instinct — listen first, gather real information, then adjust — is exactly how I will approach living and working in Brazil. I will not arrive assuming I understand how business, or daily life, works there; I will assume I do not, and learn by asking. I have done versions of this before: working in Baltimore's Mayor's Office on the city's opioid response, alongside grassroots groups doing the hardest work, I learned to earn trust by showing up, listening, and being useful before being certain.
>
> Practically, I adapt fast to logistics and discomfort — new cities, long hours, plans that break — and I will prepare the controllable parts, from language basics to local context, so my energy on the ground goes to the people and the questions, not the friction.

## Ingested advice — Fulbright Brazil + writing craft (2026-08-20)
Distilled from `raw/assets/`: two Fulbright Brazil YouTube live sessions (Study/Research + ETA) and the FulbrightMENA "how to write a good application" piece. See also [[fulbright-country-selection]].

**Program facts (Study/Research, Brazil):** ~30 grants/yr · 9 months · follows US academic calendar (starts ~Sept) · 14 days vacation · **no flex — full 9-month commitment** · all fields accepted · applicant finds their own host; **a host letter is part of the application** and selection explicitly weighs *why* that host + location were chosen · student visa (VITEM), multiple entries; grant is full-time, travel must be cleared with the commission.

**Strategy implications for us:**
1. **Recife/Northeast = advantage.** Fulbright Brazil gives **preference to hosts/projects outside São Paulo, Rio, and Salvador**; big-city projects need strong justification. Our "Northeast, historically underserved" framing is exactly what they reward — lean in.
2. **Host = CESAR (decided 2026-08-20).** The grantee advice was "pick a prestigious adviser whose name opens doors," but a host that *actually agrees to host* beats a bigger name that hasn't replied. CESAR has engaged; anchor the SGP on CESAR, keep Porto Digital / CIn-UFPE as ecosystem context.
3. **Language = advanced-Spanish route (Cole, confirmed 2026-08-20).** Study/Research allows **intermediate Portuguese OR advanced Spanish by grant start (Sept 2027)**, not by deadline. Cole has **advanced Spanish** → satisfies the requirement. Proof = a **written + spoken evaluation by an academic/native speaker** (no standardized test). Still add a **light Portuguese ramp** for daily life (grantee tip: **Pimsleur > Duolingo**, ~$10/mo). State this in the SGP + language self-eval.
4. **Reciprocity + community side project weighted heavily.** Frame around what Cole will **contribute to and learn from** Brazilian peers, not career advancement. Plan concrete community engagement (soccer, volunteer teaching, sharing AI/US-startup knowledge). Our Community Engagement answer already aligns.

**Writing craft (all sources):** center on a **true passion** told as a **story** (how the interest formed → how pursued → how Fulbright advances it); weave achievements in, don't lead with grades; **avoid jargon + idioms** (US *and* Brazilian readers); keep the project analytically sound but *readable*; get feedback; read the award description more than once; short answers are the hardest part.

**Timeline:** IIE manages the form; Brazil commission does final selection. Semifinal notice ~Dec; results ~March/April.
