### 3. Abstract / Summary of Proposal *(1750 char max, no links)*

**🇧🇷 Brazil** — 1,674 chars

> For most of history, founding a company demanded three scarce resources: capital to build before earning revenue, expertise across engineering, design, law, and market research, and infrastructure to reach customers. Where those were hardest to assemble, most would-be founders simply could not start. Artificial intelligence is now collapsing all three barriers at once: a single founder can prototype software, draft contracts, run market research, and reach customers with tools that cost almost nothing.
>
> My project asks where, and for whom, that unlock matters most, and how it is reshaping who gets to become an entrepreneur in Brazil's emerging economy. I will base the work in Recife, in the Northeast, where a fast-growing digital economy anchored by CESAR and the Porto Digital ecosystem is developing outside the traditional São Paulo–Rio axis, an ideal setting to study entrepreneurship in a historically underserved region.
>
> Over nine months, affiliated with CESAR, I will: (1) embed with founders, incubatees, and faculty to observe how early-stage ventures use AI day to day; (2) interview founders, investors, and support-organization leaders on which barrier, capital, expertise, or infrastructure, AI relaxes most, and what new bottlenecks appear; and (3) build case studies of ventures that could not plausibly have started five years ago, tracing exactly which AI capabilities made them viable. I will synthesize the findings into a written analysis shared back with the host community.
>
> I bring a practitioner's lens: I work at Johns Hopkins Technology Ventures on the venture-capital and translational-funding team, and I co-founded a student venture.
