# NEUROSCI 206 — Chemical Senses: Olfaction & Taste (Week 7)

Week 7 knowledge note for [[neurosci-206]] — **Unit 1 (Sensation)**, textbook *Neuroscience* 7e **Ch 14 (olfaction) + Ch 15 (taste)**.

## The two systems at a glance (the contrasts that get tested)
| | **Olfaction (smell)** | **Gustation (taste)** |
|---|---|---|
| Stimulus | **airborne** odorant molecules | **dissolved/ingested** tastants |
| Receptor cell | olfactory receptor neuron (**ORN**) — a true neuron, CN I | taste receptor cell (**Type II / III**) — *not* a neuron |
| Transduction | **GPCR → Golf → cAMP → CNG cation channel** | Type II: **GPCR → Ca²⁺ from ER → TRPM5**; Type III: **ionotropic (sour)** |
| Cranial nerve | **CN I** (olfactory) | **CN VII, IX, X** |
| First CNS stop | **olfactory bulb** (telencephalon) | **nucleus of the solitary tract** (medulla) |
| Thalamus? | **NO — only sense that bypasses thalamus** | **YES — VPM parvocellular** |
| Primary cortex | **piriform (olfactory) cortex** | **insula / frontal operculum** |
| Coding | combinatorial (periphery) → **temporal** (cortex) | labeled-line + across-neuron pattern → **temporal** (cortex) |
| Regenerates? | **YES — ORNs from basal cells** (only regenerating primary sensory neuron) | **YES — taste cells from basal cells** |

**Flavor** = olfaction + gustation + trigeminal somatosensation + audition + vision, integrated in the **orbitofrontal cortex**.

## OLFACTION (Ch 14)

### 14.1 — The olfactory (+ vomeronasal) systems process airborne molecules that influence a wide range of behaviors
- Airborne molecules → **(a) olfactory** sensations (olfactory system) + **(b) noxious** sensations (trigeminal chemosensory system, in nose/mouth/eyes). Ingested molecules → **gustatory** sensations.
- Chemical senses drive **nutrition, physiology (visceral/reproductive/parental), social behavior, safety, hedonic reward** — powerful motivators.
- **Accessory olfactory system** (many mammals, *not* functional humans): **vomeronasal organ (VNO)** in nasal septum → **accessory olfactory bulb** → limbic forebrain. Detects:
  - **Pheromones** = chemicals for **conspecific** (same-species) interactions (mating, parental, territory marking).
  - **Kairomones** = chemicals for **cross-species** interactions (predator/prey).
  - In **humans the VNO is absent / accessory bulb vestigial** → non-functional; human social olfactory effects, if any, go through the **main** bulb.

### 14.2 — Olfactory transduction occurs via G-protein-coupled receptors
**Cascade (memorize the order):**
1. Odorant binds a **GPCR** on the **cilia** of the ORN (cilia extend into the mucus layer).
2. Activates **Golf** (olfactory-specific G-protein).
3. Golf → **adenylyl cyclase** → ↑ **cAMP**.
4. cAMP opens a **cation (CNG) channel** → **Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ influx** → **depolarization**.
5. Ca²⁺ opens a **Ca²⁺-gated Cl⁻ channel** → **Cl⁻ efflux** → *amplifies* the depolarization.
6. If threshold reached → **AP at the base of the ORN** → transmitted via **olfactory nerve (CN I)** to the bulb.
- **Adaptation:** Ca²⁺ + **calmodulin** desensitizes the CNG channel to cAMP; Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger clears Ca²⁺ → receptor potential falls (why a smell fades).
- **Genetics:** ~**400** functional olfactory receptor genes (largest gene family); **each ORN expresses only one allele of one receptor gene**.
- **Regeneration:** ORNs are the **only primary sensory neuron that regenerates** (from **basal cells** / neural stem cells) and regrows axons into the brain — because the ORN sits "one cell, one synapse from the outside world" and is so vulnerable.

### 14.3 — Olfactory info relays directly to the bulb, then to multiple forebrain targets
- Odorants → **olfactory epithelium** → ORN axons through the **cribriform plate** → synapse in the **olfactory bulb**.
  - ⚠️ The **olfactory bulb is a telencephalic (forebrain) structure, NOT a nerve**; the **lateral olfactory tract** (its output) is a brain tract, not a cranial nerve.
- Bulb projection neurons = **mitral cells** → **lateral olfactory tract** → **olfactory cortex** on ventromedial forebrain:
  - **piriform cortex** (temporal/frontal junction) · **olfactory tubercle** (part of ventral striatum) · **cortical amygdala** · **entorhinal cortex** (hippocampal formation → memory).
- Onward: thalamus (mediodorsal nucleus), hypothalamus, **orbital/medial PFC**.
- **Two exceptional features (high-yield):**
  1. **Only sensory system that does NOT relay through the thalamus before reaching cortex.**
  2. Olfactory cortex has **no known topographic map** of the stimulus or epithelium.

### 14.4 — Olfactory capacity reflects the size/complexity of the system
- **Combinatorial code** (NOT labeled-line): one receptor can respond to several odorants, one odorant activates several receptors → identity = the *combination* of activated receptors encoding **molecular shape**. (Stereoisomers differ: **D-carvone = caraway, L-carvone = spearmint**; low indole = floral, high = putrid.)
- **Convergence in the bulb:** all ~**25,000 ORNs expressing the same receptor** converge onto a **small set of bilaterally symmetric glomeruli** → maximizes sensitivity/fidelity. Glomeruli also contain **tufted, periglomerular, and granule cells**; **granule cells = lateral inhibition** across mitral cells + plasticity.
- **Sparse coding** in the bulb; **temporal coding** in cortex — a single odor activates **broadly distributed** cortical neurons (convergence is lost), and central structures **oscillate** (near-synchronous firing). No spatial map → **temporal code**.
- **Clinical:**
  - **COVID-19 anosmia:** SARS-CoV-2 binds **ACE-2** on **sustentacular (support) cells** → impairs support of ORN cilia + down-regulates odorant-receptor genes. (ORNs themselves largely spared → why smell usually returns.)
  - **Head trauma:** brain movement **shears CN I axons at the cribriform plate** → anosmia; partial recovery via ORN regrowth (not always 100%).
  - Olfactory decline is an **early sign of neurodegenerative/psychiatric disease** (Parkinson's, Alzheimer's) and of normal aging.

## TASTE / GUSTATION (Ch 15)

### 15.1 — Taste qualities are detected by specialized taste-bud cells, conveyed by 3 cranial nerves
- **5 taste qualities:** sweet, salty, sour, bitter, **umami** (amino acids / "savory").
- **Taste buds** (1,000–5,000; big interindividual variation) sit on the tongue, soft palate, pharynx, upper esophagus. On the tongue they cluster in **papillae:**
  - **Circumvallate + foliate** (back) = large, **100s of buds each**.
  - **Fungiform** (front/rest) = small, **<20 buds each**.
- **Cell types (high-yield — don't confuse II vs III):**
  - **Type I** = glia-like **support** cells (clear neurotransmitter).
  - **Type II** = **sweet / bitter / umami** (and some salty); **GPCR**-based; release **ATP** via a **non-vesicular "channel synapse."**
  - **Type III** = **sour** (and some salty); **ionotropic**; release **serotonin** via **conventional Ca²⁺-dependent vesicular exocytosis (chemical synapse).**
  - Each receptor cell expresses only **1–2 receptor categories** (specialized).
- Taste cells are **not neurons** → they synapse onto **gustatory afferent axons**. **Basal cells** regenerate taste cells throughout life. (Trigeminal nerve carries general somatic sensation of the tongue — separate from taste.)

**Transduction detail:**
- **Type II (sweet/bitter/umami):** GPCR → G-protein → **Ca²⁺ released from ER** → opens **TRPM5** → Na⁺ in / K⁺ out → depolarize → (reinforced by voltage-gated Na⁺) → voltage-gated channel releases **ATP**. *Bitter* receptors act alone; *sweet/umami* receptors **dimerize**.
- **Type III (sour):** **protons** from the acid diffuse in + **block a K⁺ channel** → depolarize → voltage-gated Na⁺ & Ca²⁺ open → Ca²⁺ triggers **serotonin** vesicle release.

### 15.2 — Gustatory info flows through diverging/converging pathways; spatio-temporal coding
**Pathway (memorize nerves → nucleus → thalamus → cortex):**
1. **CN VII (facial)** → anterior tongue (+ soft palate); **CN IX (glossopharyngeal)** → posterior tongue; **CN X (vagus)** → epiglottis/esophagus.
2. → **solitary tract** → **rostral nucleus of the solitary tract (NST)** in the medulla. (Caudal NST = **visceral** sensory relay; integration drives protective reflexes like **gag/vomit**.)
3. → **VPM, parvocellular division** of the thalamus (small-celled VPMpc).
4. → **rostral insula** (primary gustatory cortex, in the lateral fissure) → interconnected with **posterior orbitofrontal cortex** (where taste + smell + somatic + visual = **flavor** + hedonic value).
- **Detection thresholds:** higher (less sensitive) for dietary staples (**salts, carbs — millimolar**); lower (more sensitive) for likely toxins (**bitter alkaloids — nanomolar**). More concentration → more cells/firing → more intensity.
- **Two coding theories:**
  - **Labeled-line:** dedicated receptor→pathway→cortex lines per quality (holds *subcortically*, to a degree).
  - **Across-neuron pattern (population code):** afferents branch across cell types; convergence/divergence at each stage → identity = **pattern across many non-specialist neurons**.
- **Temporal code in gustatory cortex:** like olfaction, **no strict topographic map**; signals **broadly distributed**; info carried by **spike timing** + **multiphasic firing-rate time course**.

### 15.3 — Flavor involves olfaction, somatosensation, audition, and vision
- **Flavor ≠ taste.** Flavor = multimodal integration, chiefly in **orbitofrontal cortex**.
- **Olfaction:** **orthonasal** (sniffing in) vs **retronasal** (from mouth up the nasopharynx). *(Tutorial note's framing: orthonasal is called more important for food experience — a slightly non-standard claim, flag it if the RA tests it.)* Pinch-your-nose demo = food goes bland.
- **Trigeminal (somatosensory) contributions:** **capsaicin → "hot," menthol → "cool,"** astringency (tannins in wine/tea precipitating proteins), dehydration. These C-polymodal trigeminal afferents can also branch into the **rostral NST**.
- **Audition + vision:** sight and sound of food (crunch, plating) shape perceived flavor; integrated via OFC input from higher auditory/visual cortex.

### 15.4 — Palatability/aversiveness is set by gustatory + limbic systems and modified by experience
- **Hedonic value** = pleasantness/aversiveness; integrates multimodal signal + prior experience; read out in **facial expressions**.
- **Liking/disliking circuitry:** **medial + orbital PFC, insula, amygdala, ventral basal ganglia (ventral striatum)** — all **modulated by dopamine from the VTA (ventral tegmental area)**.
- **Plasticity:** tastes and aversions are **learned** (acquired taste for coffee; **conditioned taste aversion** after "food poisoning"; social learning). Synaptic/circuit plasticity underlies the change.
- **Clinical:** **anhedonia** (loss of pleasure, incl. food) in depression/disease; **Alzheimer's** impairs the multimodal integration behind hedonic value.

## Study questions (professor's own — memorize)
1. **Most accurate re: olfactory encoding?** → **(D)** Central olfactory structures use a **temporal code** — near-synchronous oscillations broadly distributed when an odorant is present. *(Not labeled-line; combinatorial is at the periphery, not cortex.)*
2. **Correct tastant-receptor ↔ cell-type pairing?** → **(D)** **Type II cells: GPCRs** sensitive to **sweet, bitter, or umami.**
3. **Correct neurotransmitter ↔ cell-type pairing? (pick two)** → **(B)** Type II: **ATP** via **channel synapse** (non-vesicular) + **(D)** Type III: **serotonin** via **Ca²⁺-dependent exocytosis** at chemical synapses.
4. **Most accurate re: gustatory encoding? (pick two)** → **(B)** subcortical processing implements a **labeled-line code** (responses reflect receptor properties) + **(D)** higher stages (insula) use a **temporal code** for complex mixtures.
5. **What influences the hedonic value of a meal?** → **(E) all of the above** — multimodal + prior experience, learned associations, VTA dopamine, and disease states.
